Every site in the proposal enrolled fewer than 400 students with building use under 65%, and none is projected to gain enrollment. The 14 elementary sites averaged 307 students and 51.3% occupancy in 2025–26; only Anderson and Dolores Huerta cleared 60%.

The list touches all seven school board districts and six of seven feeder patterns. Edison is the exception: every Edison school sat at 75% occupancy or above, two of them above 95%.

        Other building changes

          How sites were chosen

          Three primary criteria: student enrollment, building size and use, and student outcomes. Supporting data: enrollment trends, safe walking paths, unique programs, student demographics and needs, retention and transfer history, travel distance, nearby options, and physical barriers such as railroads and highways.

          Boundary changes favored the shortest distance to a welcoming school with 85% or less building use after the move, and avoided crossing railroads and major roads.

          Budget and enrollment context

          The board's 27 July resolution requires at least $12 million in savings by 19 October. The proposal counts $11.1 million from closures ($8.5 million in staffing allocations, $2.6 million in operating costs, plus $0.9 million deferred) and $1.5 million from central office reductions.

          TPS reports enrollment down 779 students from a year earlier as of 18 September 2026, and cites an Esri projection of a 2.5% decline in children over the next five years. It states that about 2,600 additional students would be needed to balance the budget at current staffing levels.

          Of 697 community survey responses, 65% chose fewer schools with more consistent services over more schools with reduced services (18% more schools, 16% unsure).

          Staffing ratios stay at 24:1 (K–2), 28:1 (3–5), 29:1 (middle) and 33:1 (high). Bond funds flagged for closing sites would be redirected to receiving sites through the bond oversight committee.

          School list

          The 17 closures and the building changes come from slide 27 of the TPS board presentation Superintendent's Proposal to Address Budget Deficit (14 elementary, 2 middle and 1 alternative site), presented at the 21 September 2026 board meeting. Consolidation groups, welcoming sites, students and families affected, feeder shifts, building use by feeder, selection criteria and the budget figures come from slides 7–49 of the same deck.

          Feeder-pattern membership for all 85 sites: TPS Our Schools pages (tulsaschools.org/enrollment/our-schools), feeder headings, read 22 September 2026.

          Addresses for all 85 mapped campuses: Tulsa Public Schools Directory of Schools, 2026–2027, the district's printable directory, cross-checked against individual school site footers on tulsaschools.org.

          Coordinates

          U.S. Census Bureau Geocoder, Public_AR_Current benchmark, one address at a time. Every point on this map is a geocoder response, not an estimate — with one exception: the 3100 block of W Edison St has no address range in Census TIGER, so the Central Middle / Central High campus uses the published coordinate from the English Wikipedia article Central High School (Tulsa, Oklahoma).

          Each campus is assigned to its census tract by point-in-polygon. Greenwood Leadership Academy, Central and S.O.A.R. fall in Osage County: west Tulsa north of the river lies across the county line.

          Income and boundaries

          Tract figures: ACS 2024 five-year, tables B19013 (median household income), B01003 (population) and B17020 (poverty status), from the Census Bureau API. Tract boundaries: Census cartographic boundary files (500k), 2022 vintage.

          ZIP figures: ACS 2024 five-year as republished by IncomeByZipCode.com. ZIP boundaries: 2010 Census ZCTAs. ZCTAs approximate postal ZIPs, so a few campuses sit a few metres across a line from the ZIP the district lists.

          Base map: CARTO Positron / Dark Matter vector tiles on OpenStreetMap data, rendered with MapLibre GL JS.

          Site detail and process

          Feeder pattern, enrollment, occupancy, bond-history and art/music staffing figures: Tulsa World, “18 schools listed in draft TPS closure, repurposing proposal,” 21 September 2026, drawing on TPS 2025–26 building-capacity and enrollment data and open records requests. The World's 18 is the 17 closure sites plus Central High School, which is repurposed rather than closed.

          Superintendent Ebony Johnson announced the consolidation review on 8 September 2026, seeking $12 million in cuts; board votes were scheduled for 21 September, 5 October and 19 October 2026 (Tulsa Flyer, 18 September 2026).

          What's next (slide 52): community survey open through 16 October; information sessions 29 September, 6 October, 7 October (Spanish), 8 October, 12 October and 14 October 2026; details at tulsaschools.org/planning. Before winter break the district says it will answer questions on calendar and bell times, transportation, home-school assignments, transfers and before/after-school care.
